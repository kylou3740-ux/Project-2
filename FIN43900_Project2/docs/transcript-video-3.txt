Hello, and good evening. This is my Investment decision application. I used this application to evaluate several portfolio construction approaches against a defined investment mandate. I did use AI tools for this— I used AI for things like coding, troubleshooting, some documentation. I reviewed and tested all of the work. Oh, and this is an educational project, not investment advice.
The application uses historical ETF price data and a selected cutoff date. We used an investment signal of a twelve-month momentum measure that skips the most recent month. The signal ranks the risk sleeves, and the application goes ahead and selects the two highest-ranked assets for the constrained portfolio. It's a relatively simple, pretty transparent kind of signal; on the other hand, historical performance does not necessarily mean that its future performance will match that.
For this, I compared three portfolio approaches. The first is an equal-weight baseline, the second uses inverse volatility to allocate based on estimated risk, and the third is a constrained momentum portfolio. The constrained portfolio uses the momentum rankings to select its risk sleeves and then evaluates feasible allocations using the return hurdle and minimum reserve requirement. I evaluated the strategies using annualized historical return, volatility, and maximum drawdown. While these metrics do help compare historical outcomes, they are sample-dependent. So I can’t say that they should be used as like a forecast or guarantee. I also conducted a stress test by increasing the assumed implementation cost from zero to fifty basis points. As shown in our recorded results, the estimated annualized returns declined by approximately ten basis points across the three strategies, while the rounded volatility, drawdown, and constrained allocation changed very little. This suggests that the cost assumption affected the estimated return, but the comparison is retrospective and may also reflect differences in the data cutoff, so I would not interpret it as a perfectly isolated causal test.

The application also allows me to change the estimation window, return assumptions, reserve minimum, and return hurdle to examine how sensitive the results are to different inputs
The application also evaluates the portfolios against the coded mandate checks, including whether the weights sum to one hundred percent, whether the portfolio is long-only, and whether the reserve minimum is satisfied. However, passing these checks doesn’t immediately mean the entire mandate has been satisfied.
There are several things that aren’t quite verified yet, like the ETF look-through issuer concentration, five-business-day liquidity, annual turnover, et cetera. Since these requirements are not fully verified, I would not consider this application to be enough on its own to approve a portfolio for full implementation.
My recommendation is to pilot the strategy and defer full adoption. The momentum signal is transparent and relatively simple, we can’t be sure of its ability to generate returns above the broader market. And also, several mandate requirements remain unverified. If the committee were to consider a pilot, I would use an illustrative range of zero to ten percent of reserve assets, subject to committee approval, further data verification, and completion of the outstanding mandate checks. This range just simply represents my judgment for further evaluation

I would reconsider or suspend the strategy if the data cannot be reproduced, a hard mandate requirement is breached, the optimizer becomes infeasible, actual costs exceed the stress assumption, or the allocations change materially under reasonable changes in assumptions. Overall, I would use this application as a decision-support tool, rather than as an automatic investment decision.
